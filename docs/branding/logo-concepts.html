Brand exploration

Socratic Trade — fourteen logo directions

Concepts for the wordmark and mark, built on the product's existing palette: ink #0f1722, green #0e9f6e on light, mint #63e6be on dark. Every concept is shown on both grounds.

The first group keeps the words as the logo — quiet, type-led, with at most one small device. The second group adds a mark that can stand alone as a favicon or app icon. Round 2 (top) distills your Adobe Firefly comps — candlestick letterforms, the owl, market red/green — into disciplined, professional versions.

Round 2 — refined from the Firefly comps

What the Firefly images got right: candles as brand DNA, Athena's owl for "Socratic", honest red/green. What they got wrong: every letter made of dozens of candles. These keep the ideas and cut the noise — each uses the candlestick exactly once, deliberately.

K

The Owl — two candles

The owl from your comps, rebuilt from exactly two candlesticks: wicks become ear tufts and feet, bodies become the face, one amber beak. It's Athena's owl made of the market — and it survives at 16 px. My pick for the primary identity.

L

The Seal

The professional version of the circular Firefly comps: a proper seal — name set on the arc, owl at center. Works as an avatar, a watermark on reports, or an "exchange crest" moment on the landing page.

M

The Candle-I

The letters-made-of-candles idea, disciplined to a single substitution: the I of SOCRATIC is one green candle. The words stay perfectly legible and fill the logo — the closest Round-2 option to your "words clear, not busy" brief.

N

The Cluster

The only concept that keeps the red: three candles — up, down, up — rising left to right. Honest about how markets actually move (the product loses trades too, and explains why), with the red held to one small, deliberate note.

Wordmark-led

The name does the work. These stay legible at any size and are the least busy options.

A

Full Stop — Socratic-Trade

The domain itself is the logo. One geometric green dot separates a bold "Socratic" from a light "Trade" — the period reads as both the URL and the moment of decision. The single quietest option here.

B

Inscription

Wide-tracked serif capitals, like lettering cut in stone — the classical, philosophical read without resorting to a Greek column. "TRADE" sits small in green beneath a hairline rule.

C

Dialogue

Set like a line of printed Plato: the green quotation dash opens "Socratic", and "Trade" answers on the next line, closing with a green full stop. The staggered layout is the Socratic method — statement and reply.

D

Trendline

The full name in steady letterspaced caps over one thin rising line ending in a price dot — a direct, quieter descendant of the current favicon. Words dominate; the market is a single stroke.

E

Delta

Heavy "SOCRATIC" stacked over a letterspaced "TRADE" whose A is the Greek delta — change, the thing being traded. The most conventional of the type-led set, but sturdy and instantly legible.

Mark-led

Each of these carries a symbol that works alone at 16 px — favicon, app icon, avatar — with the wordmark alongside.

F

Open Question

A question mark whose dot is a green candlestick — questioning ends in a position. The tightest fusion of "Socratic" and "trade" in one glyph, and a strong standalone app icon.

G

Sigma

One stroke that is three things at once: a Greek sigma, the S of Socratic, and a zigzag price path ending at a green tick. Geometric, ownable, and unmistakably Greek without being decorative.

H

Argument

A dialogue bubble holding a rising line: the product in one picture — it argues about markets, then acts. Friendliest of the marks; suits the coaching and explain-your-reasoning surfaces.

I

Monogram — S.T

Serif "S.T" with the green period from concept A carried into a compact monogram. The "S.T" crops perfectly into a square avatar; the full name sits beneath in small caps for lockups.

J

Continuity

The existing favicon, kept as-is and given a proper wordmark for the first time. Zero rebrand risk: this is what shipping a lockup around the mark users already see would look like.

These are concept comps: type is set live in Helvetica/Georgia stacks so it stays editable. Whichever direction wins gets redrawn with final letterforms outlined, plus favicon, app-icon, and mono variants. From Round 2 I'd pick K (the candlestick owl) as the identity, with M as the type-led alternative; from Round 1, A, D, F, and G remain the shortlist — each survives a 16 px favicon and a black-and-white fax test.
